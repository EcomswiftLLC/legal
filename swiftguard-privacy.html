Privacy policy

SwiftGuard: Virus Scanner & PC Security for Windows, by Ecom Swift LLC. Last updated 31 October 2026.

In short

  • SwiftGuard checks your PC on your PC. Settings, scan history, quarantine, reports and logs stay on your device, in SwiftGuard's own app data folder.
  • No account, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. SwiftGuard does not upload your files, file names, scan results or system information to Ecom Swift LLC or anyone else.
  • SwiftGuard itself makes no internet connections. The only online parts are Microsoft Store purchases and licence checks, which Windows and the Microsoft Store handle, and web pages that open in your browser when you click a link.
  • SwiftGuard works beside Microsoft Defender. Microsoft Defender Antivirus, which is part of Windows, does the scanning; SwiftGuard does not replace it.

What the app reads

To show your protection status and run the checks you ask for, SwiftGuard reads information that Windows already keeps on your PC: Microsoft Defender status, settings and detection history, Windows Security Center, Windows Firewall, User Account Control, Windows Update, security settings for the Security Checkup, processor, memory, drive and battery readings for the Performance Check, your startup apps, the list of files in your Downloads folder, and the files and folders you choose to scan. File contents are read only to scan them or to compute a checksum (SHA-256), and are never sent anywhere.

With SwiftGuard Pro, depending on the tools you turn on, it also reads: startup entries, scheduled tasks, automatic services, the hosts file and Defender settings (to show what changed), files in folders you choose to watch, which apps used your camera, microphone or location (from Windows' own privacy records), network adapters, Wi-Fi security type, firewall rules and open ports on this PC, disk health and the busiest programs at the time of a health check. These readings stay on your PC.

What the app stores, and where

Everything is stored in SwiftGuard's app data folder on your PC (for the Microsoft Store version, the folder Windows gives the app for its own data):

  • your settings, including the language and notification choices;
  • scan history (time, type, engine, result, counts) and, for items a scan found, the file path, detection name and checksum;
  • quarantine: files you chose to quarantine, encrypted so that only your Windows account on this PC can open them, and their index;
  • a security history of protection status changes, cleanups and startup changes;
  • with Pro tools turned on: your scan schedules, earlier readings used to show changes (startup items, tasks, services, hosts file, Defender settings, watched folders), PC health readings, alerts, an automation and quarantine activity log;
  • a saved copy of your Microsoft Store licence check, protected for your Windows account, so Pro keeps working for a short time when the Store cannot be reached;
  • log files for troubleshooting. Logs do not contain file contents or passwords, and your user name and profile path are masked.

You can delete scan history, quarantine items and history entries in the app.

Background helper and scheduled scans (Pro)

If you turn on a Pro background tool (scheduled scans, background download checks, monitoring and alerts, health checks or automation rules), SwiftGuard starts a small helper that keeps running when the window is closed and starts when you sign in to Windows. It does the same local work described above and shows its results as Windows notifications. It is listed in Windows Settings under Apps > Startup. Turning every background tool off stops it.

Your files and cleanup

Scans only look; SwiftGuard does not remove anything by itself. It quarantines, restores or deletes a file only when you choose to, or when you turned on an automation rule that quarantines new threats and confirmed it. PC Cleanup shows a preview first and deletes only after you confirm. Microsoft Defender may still act on files using its own settings; that is Defender, not SwiftGuard.

Reports, diagnostics and feedback

Reports (PDF or CSV) and scan exports are created only when you choose to save them, in the place you pick. Export diagnostics shows you the whole file first and saves it where you choose; nothing is sent. If you choose to send feedback, SwiftGuard opens your own email app with a message to support@ecomswiftllc.com that shows the app version and Windows version. You see it before you send it, and nothing else is attached unless you add it.

Purchases

SwiftGuard Pro is bought through the Microsoft Store. Microsoft handles payment, renewal, cancellation and your payment details; SwiftGuard never sees or stores them. The app asks the Microsoft Store, through Windows, which SwiftGuard Pro licence your Microsoft account has, and shows the Store's prices. Microsoft's privacy statement applies to the Microsoft Store.

Optional third-party scanner

If you install ClamAV yourself, SwiftGuard can run it on your PC for folder, file and drive scans. SwiftGuard does not include, download or update ClamAV; keeping its signatures up to date is up to you and ClamAV's own updater.

Removing SwiftGuard

Uninstalling SwiftGuard removes its app data folder, including settings, history, logs and the quarantine (quarantined files cannot be restored after that, so restore any you need first). Files you saved yourself, such as exported reports or diagnostics, stay where you saved them.

Children

SwiftGuard does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
