Tattoo Try On: Inkaura: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Inkaura is a tattoo try-on app made by Ecom Swift LLC. It shows how a tattoo design or lettering could look on a photo of your own skin. You don't need an account to use it. Your photos are processed on your phone and are never uploaded to us. We don't run a server that receives your pictures, and we don't sell personal information.

Your photos

  • You choose what Inkaura sees: the app opens only the photos you pick with Android's photo picker or take with your phone's camera app. It does not browse your gallery. You can also use the skin canvas, a forearm picture drawn by the app, without any photo.
  • Everything happens on your device: placing, shaping, ink blending, erasing, the tattoo card and the stencil are all processed locally.
  • Skin tone and limb direction: to blend ink, the app samples the colour of the skin under the design and reads which way an arm or leg runs in the photo. These values are calculated on the phone, used only for the picture you are editing, and stored only inside your project.
  • On-device pose detection: if you tap Find limbs in photo, Inkaura uses a Google ML Kit pose model that ships inside the app to find shoulders, arms and legs and suggest a spot. It runs on your phone; the picture is not sent anywhere for it, the points are not stored, and nothing is used to recognise or identify anyone. Inkaura does not detect faces and does not estimate age, gender or any other personal trait.
  • What gets saved: when you tap Save, the finished picture, tattoo card or stencil is written to Pictures/Inkaura on your phone. If you tap Share, it is handed to the app you pick. Inkaura keeps your projects (a working copy of the photo, a small preview and your layer settings) in its private storage so you can continue later, together with your favourites and settings. You can delete one project or all of them in the app; uninstalling the app removes everything.

Permissions

Inkaura asks for no camera, contacts, location, microphone or storage permission on current Android versions. The Take a photo button opens your phone's own camera app, and the photo it takes is handed to Inkaura. On Android 9 and older Inkaura asks for permission to save to your Pictures folder. It uses the internet only for ads and Google Play purchases.

Advertising

Inkaura is free and shows ads from Google AdMob. Before ads load, a consent form appears where the law requires one (for example in the European Economic Area). AdMob can use your device's advertising ID and general device information, such as model, language and app version, to show ads and measure them. Banners appear on the home, design library, save and settings screens, never over the photo while you edit. A full-screen ad may appear after you save a picture, and it is limited in number. A rewarded video plays only when you choose to watch one to use Premium features for 24 hours.

You can change your consent at any time in Settings, under Ad privacy choices (shown where the law requires it). Google's own privacy policy applies to the ads it serves: policies.google.com/privacy.

Purchases

Inkaura Premium (a monthly or yearly subscription, or a one-time lifetime purchase) and the one-time Remove ads purchase are sold through Google Play Billing. Google handles the payment; we never see your card or payment details. The app only learns which products your Google account owns, so it can unlock them. Premium removes all ads.

Ratings

After you have saved a few pictures, the app may ask whether you are enjoying it. If you choose to rate, Google Play's own review sheet opens; the review is handled by Google Play.

Data sent to Google services

The ad service, Google Play Billing and Google Play services may collect approximate location, app interactions, purchase history, crash logs, diagnostics and device identifiers, as described in Google's policies. We do not receive this data about you as an individual.

Children

Inkaura is made for adults. It is not directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
